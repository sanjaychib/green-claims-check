1 · The rules in brief

What you are checking against

EU Directive 2024/825

Empowering consumers for the green transition

Applies from 27 September 2026 to commercial practices aimed at EU consumers, including Swiss companies selling into the EU. It adds these to the list of practices that are always unfair:

  • generic claims such as “eco-friendly” without recognised excellent performance
  • neutral or reduced emission claims based on offsetting
  • sustainability labels not based on a certification scheme or public authority
  • presenting legal requirements as a special feature
  • claims about the whole product when they apply only to part of it
ISO 14021

Self-declared environmental claims

The international standard for claims a company makes about its own products. Claims should be accurate, specific, verifiable and not misleading. Vague terms like “environmentally friendly” or “green” should not be used.

The test is simple to state: is the claim specific, true for what it covers, and can you prove it?

4 · Take it to work

Green claim checklist

Five questions before any environmental claim goes out

  1. Is it specific? Replace general words (green, eco, sustainable, natural) with a measurable fact.
  2. Does it cover what it says? If it applies to the packaging only, say packaging.
  3. Can we prove it today? Name the document that backs it up: test report, certificate, supplier data.
  4. Does it rely on offsetting? Then do not call the product neutral or reduced. Report actual reductions and offsetting separately.
  5. Is it just the law? Do not present a legal requirement as a special feature. Use only labels from certification schemes or public authorities.

Based on EU Directive 2024/825 and ISO 14021. Summary for training use, not legal advice.
